# Daily Paper Digest — 2026-05-08

## Theme
Today’s worthwhile papers were about **what kind of structure actually helps planning and control**. The strongest work either (i) changes the planner-facing state itself, (ii) tests whether a representation preserves action-relevant semantics rather than just pixels, or (iii) gives a cleaner mathematical object for long-horizon composition.

## Short overview
This was a **selective four-paper day**. I screened a wider batch from the May 7 arXiv drop, but most candidates were either too slogan-heavy, too benchmark-local, or too thin on mechanism.

The best paper today is **HaM-World**. It makes a concrete claim about what planner-facing latent structure should look like: selective memory for approximate Markov completeness plus a soft Hamiltonian split between canonical dynamics and non-conservative context.

**Reconstruction or Semantics?** is the most practically useful paper if the immediate question is what latent space a robotic diffusion world model should actually use. It is an evaluation paper, not a new world-model architecture, but it asks the right question and answers it with a reasonably disciplined protocol.

**Hitting Time Isomorphism for Multi-Stage Planning with Foundation Policies** is more theoretical and less directly plug-and-play, but it has the cleanest conceptual payoff: if you want multi-stage planning to compose, the representation should preserve directed temporal progress, not just symmetric similarity.

**EA-WM** is worth keeping as a more engineering-heavy but still meaningful mechanism paper. Its best idea is to move action conditioning into the camera-aligned visual domain instead of asking a video model to infer geometry from tiny action tokens.

   - Role: **Directly relevant**
   - Why it matters: It puts structure exactly where planning fails most: in long-horizon rollout stability, OOD robustness, and latent organization for control.

   - Role: **Direct utility + evaluation framing**

   - Verdict: **Useful**
   - Role: **Direct utility + interface design**
   - Why it matters: It treats action-to-video alignment as a representation problem instead of only a training problem.

## Most relevant to current work
**HaM-World** is the closest fit today. It is not just “physics-flavored world modeling”; it proposes a specific split between canonical latent dynamics, semantic/dissipative context, and history-conditioned memory, then evaluates that split on planning stability and OOD perturbation.

## Conceptual inspiration vs direct utility
- **Directly relevant:** HaM-World
- **Direct utility + good experimental framing:** Reconstruction or Semantics?
- **Conceptual inspiration with strong transfer value:** Hitting Time Isomorphism for Multi-Stage Planning with Foundation Policies
- **Useful but more engineering-heavy than conceptually deep:** EA-WM
- **Sounded relevant but not strong enough to keep:** Render, Don’t Decode; TriRelVLA; Earth-o1

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact
- **World-model structure claims:** HaM-World is useful if you need a stronger answer to “what planner-facing structure should a latent world model preserve?”
- **Representation selection:** Reconstruction or Semantics? is a good citation against choosing world models by visual fidelity alone.
- **Long-horizon compositional planning:** Hitting Time Isomorphism is useful if you want to argue that symmetric latent similarity is the wrong primitive for staged planning.
- **Action-conditioning interfaces:** EA-WM is a solid reference for lifting control into spatially grounded visual condition spaces.
- **Novelty threat:** Low direct novelty collision. The bigger value is framing, evaluation taste, and a few concrete design interfaces worth stealing.

## One-paragraph takeaway
The shared lesson today is that useful structure is the kind that the planner or controller is forced to use. **HaM-World** makes that explicit by giving planning a latent state with role-separated geometry and memory. **Reconstruction or Semantics?** shows that if the latent space preserves action-relevant semantics, downstream policy evaluation improves even when images look slightly worse. **Hitting Time Isomorphism** says multi-stage planning needs a directed notion of progress, not just closeness. **EA-WM** moves action guidance into the same spatial domain as video generation so the model does less implicit cross-domain guessing. Across all four, the key standard is the same: structure should change controllability, composition, or rollout faithfulness—not merely decorate the architecture.
