# Daily Paper Digest — 2026-05-01

## Theme
Today’s strongest theme was **explicit structure as a diagnostic or executable interface rather than a branding exercise**. The papers worth keeping either (1) learn world dynamics as modular symbolic laws under harsh data constraints, (2) test whether reasoning failures are really representation failures, (3) make simulation-ready articulated structure directly generatable from perception, or (4) explain why asymmetric data distributions can make composition learnable in the first place.

## Short overview
This was a **good, selective day**. I screened a wider set, but only **four papers felt worth saving**, and only **two feel especially strong**.

The best direct fit is **One Life to Learn**. It is one of the cleaner recent attempts to learn an executable symbolic world model from minimal, unguided interaction in a stochastic environment, which is much closer to a real scientific challenge than the usual toy deterministic setup.

The sharpest conceptual paper is **Symbolic Grounding Reveals Representational Bottlenecks in Abstract Visual Reasoning**. Its value is not a practical multimodal system; it is the diagnosis that current failure on abstract visual reasoning may be upstream of reasoning proper.

**URDF-Anything+** is the best embodiment/3D pick. It is less conceptually deep than the top two, but generating executable articulated models directly for simulation is a meaningful interface choice, not just prettier reconstruction.

I also kept **The Power of Power Law** as theory-side inspiration. It makes a non-obvious claim: for compositional tasks, flattening the skill distribution can actually make learning harder.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact
- **Symbolic world-model framing:** One Life is a strong citation against assuming symbolic world models must be deterministic, reward-shaped, or data-rich.
- **Representation-vs-reasoning framing:** Symbolic Grounding is useful if you want to argue that failure on abstract reasoning tasks may reflect poor perceptual interface design more than missing inference capacity.
- **Perception-to-simulation framing:** URDF-Anything+ is relevant for executable reconstruction arguments, especially when simulation-readiness matters more than static mesh fidelity.
- **Data-distribution framing:** The Power of Power Law is useful if you want to defend asymmetric or curriculum-like skill exposure on mechanistic rather than purely empirical grounds.
- **Novelty threat:** Low. The strongest conceptual overlap is around explicit world structure and representation bottlenecks, but none of these looks like a direct novelty collision.

## One-paragraph takeaway
The best papers today all point to the same lesson: the right intermediate structure changes what becomes learnable. One Life learns a probabilistic mixture of executable laws so world modeling stays modular even under stochasticity and tiny exploration budgets. Symbolic Grounding shows that once abstract visual problems are translated into explicit procedural structure, large parts of the apparent “reasoning failure” disappear. URDF-Anything+ treats articulated geometry plus kinematics as the generated object itself, which makes simulation the natural downstream test rather than an afterthought. The Power of Power Law makes the complementary data argument: composition may emerge more easily when training distribution asymmetry creates an easier path through the task hierarchy.
