# Daily Paper Digest — 2026-05-09

## Theme
Today’s best additions were about **explicitly modeling the thing the base model tends to hide**: a local transition kernel instead of a full simulator, a competing default completion direction instead of only stronger prompt conditioning, and symbolic task structure instead of flat transfer from one teacher.

## Short overview
This was a **selective three-paper day**. I screened more of the currently visible May 7 arXiv batch, but most remaining candidates were either too application-bound, too agent-branded, or too thin on mechanism.

The strongest paper today is **BRICKS**. It is domain-specific, but the core abstraction is excellent: learn a composable local Markov kernel with tractable likelihoods, then reuse it zero-shot on larger unseen compositions.

**DCR** is the most transferable generative-model paper today. Its key move is not “more guidance,” but identifying the model’s *preferred wrong completion* and subtracting only the aligned part of the CFG update.

**LANTERN** is weaker than the top two, but still worth keeping as a neurosymbolic transfer paper. The interesting part is not the LLM-generated automata by itself; it is the attempt to combine semantic source weighting with experience-gated teacher trust.

   - Verdict: **Highly relevant**
   - Role: **Adjacent but useful inspiration**
   - Why it matters: It shows a serious version of compositional generative modeling where locality and recursive reuse are the main story, not just branding.

2. **DCR: Counterfactual Attractor Guidance for Rare Compositional Generation**
   - Verdict: **Highly relevant**
   - Role: **Direct utility + conceptual framing**
   - Why it matters: It gives a clean inference-time mechanism for rare composition failure that is better framed than the usual “increase guidance / add constraints” toolbox.

3. **LANTERN: LLM-Augmented Neurosymbolic Transfer with Experience-Gated Reasoning Networks**
   - Verdict: **Useful**
   - Role: **Citation material + partial inspiration**
   - Why it matters: The adaptive multi-source transfer framing is more interesting than the headline LLM component, though the experimental setting still looks fairly toy-like.

## Most relevant to current work
**DCR** is the most directly reusable for current interests in compositional generation and controllability. If you care about why models miss rare but valid combinations, this is a better mechanism paper than most prompt- or attention-tweaking alternatives.

## Conceptual inspiration vs direct utility
- **Direct utility:** DCR
- **Adjacent methodological inspiration:** BRICKS
- **Mostly citation / framing material:** LANTERN
- **Sounded relevant but not strong enough to keep today:** ActCam, SkillOS, InfoCoordiBridge

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact
- **Compositional generation framing:** DCR is a useful citation if you want to argue that rare-composition failure is partly an inference-trajectory bias problem, not just a missing-conditioning problem.
- **Composable simulator design:** BRICKS is useful if you want examples of learning local reusable transition rules rather than monolithic end-to-end generators.
- **Neurosymbolic transfer:** LANTERN is more baseline/citation material than a novelty threat, but it is relevant if you need examples of semantic source weighting plus adaptive transfer gates.
- **Novelty threat:** Low direct collision. The value today is mostly reusable mechanism and framing.

## One-paragraph takeaway
The shared lesson is that better structure comes from modeling the hidden competing process directly. **BRICKS** models local physical transition kernels instead of fitting an opaque whole-simulator surrogate. **DCR** models the model’s own default completion attractor instead of only amplifying the target prompt. **LANTERN** tries to model when transferred symbolic knowledge should be trusted rather than assuming one teacher or one fixed fusion rule. Of the three, **DCR** is the clearest near-term steal; **BRICKS** is the most conceptually elegant; **LANTERN** is the most provisional.
