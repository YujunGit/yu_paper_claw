# Learning to Theorize the World from Observation

## Basic info

* Title: Learning to Theorize the World from Observation
* Authors: Doojin Baek, Gyubin Lee, Junyeob Baek, Hosung Lee, Sungjin Ahn
* Year: 2026
* Venue / source: arXiv
* Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03413
* Date surfaced: 2026-05-06
* Why selected in one sentence: It makes a serious attempt to learn executable explanatory structure from raw observations instead of treating predictive latent dynamics as sufficient understanding.

## Quick verdict

**Must read**

This is the strongest paper in today’s batch because it has both a conceptual claim and an executable mechanism. The paper argues that world understanding should be evaluated by whether a model can infer reusable latent theories, not merely compress or predict observations. That is an ambitious framing, but unlike many such papers, it is backed by a concrete probabilistic program-induction architecture and an evaluation protocol built around transfer of inferred programs.

## One-paragraph overview

The paper introduces **Learning-to-Theorize (L2T)**, a training paradigm where the target is not just next-state prediction but latent explanatory structure. Its concrete instantiation, **Neural Theorizer (NEO)**, observes paired inputs and outputs, infers a discrete latent program composed of learned primitives, and executes that program through a shared transition model to reconstruct the target. The central bet is that if primitives and executors are shared across many examples, the model will be pushed toward discovering reusable compositional transformations instead of memorizing entangled instance-specific mappings. Evaluation then asks whether an inferred program transfers to new instances generated by the same hidden mechanism, including unseen and longer compositions.

## Key questions this summary must address

### 1. What problem is the paper trying to solve?
It tries to solve a gap in current world modeling: predictive success does not necessarily imply that the model learned reusable causal or compositional structure. The paper asks whether a model can infer explicit theories of transformation from raw observations alone.

### 2. What is the method?
The method is a latent neural program-induction model. NEO encodes a source observation into a latent state, iteratively infers primitive operations with a goal-conditioned theory programmer, executes them through a shared transition model, and decodes the resulting state into the target observation. It also uses an MDL-style preference for short but accurate explanations.

### 3. What is the method motivation?
The motivation is that many existing objectives can be satisfied by entangled shortcut representations. If the goal is systematic reuse, intervention, and compositional generalization, the model should be forced to infer reusable executable structure rather than only fit input-output correlations.

### 4. What data does it use?
From the accessible text, the paper evaluates on **OTIB** (Observation-to-Theory Induction Benchmark) with at least three settings: **GridWorld**, **Arithmetic Factorization Reasoning**, and **Image Editing**. The data are observation pairs generated by latent programs, with no program supervision.

### 5. How is it evaluated?
The core evaluation is **program transferability**: infer a latent program from one observation pair, then apply that inferred program to another instance generated by the same hidden mechanism. The paper also tests unseen program compositions and longer-than-train programs.

### 6. What are the main results?
The paper reports that NEO can transfer inferred programs across instances, recombine learned primitives into unseen compositions, and generalize to longer programs than those seen during training. I did not fully audit the appendix tables, so I am taking the magnitude claims with some caution, but the evaluation target itself is the main contribution.

### 7. What is actually novel?
The novelty is not just using programs. The real contribution is combining: (1) latent executable theories learned without program supervision, (2) a shared executor that pressures primitive reuse, and (3) an evaluation protocol that tests reusable explanation rather than one-shot reconstruction.

### 8. What are the strengths?
- Strong framing: it challenges the weak equation of prediction with understanding.
- Explicit executable intermediate structure rather than post hoc interpretation.
- Good evaluation instinct: transfer the inferred theory, not only the output.
- The program-length generalization test is more meaningful than many standard OOD claims.

### 9. What are the weaknesses, limitations, or red flags?
- The current settings still appear relatively controlled and synthetic.
- The leap from latent program induction on curated benchmarks to real perceptual world modeling is large.
- Learned primitives may still be fragile or underidentified.
- “Theory” is a loaded word; the current model is closer to executable compositional transformations than broad scientific theorizing.

### 10. What challenges or open problems remain?
A major open problem is scaling this setup from clean paired observations to messy real-world video, partial observability, and noisy interventions. Another is whether the induced primitives remain interpretable and reusable when the environment becomes much richer.

### 11. What future work naturally follows?
- Apply the framework to object-centric or interaction-centric video.
- Learn hierarchical or typed primitives instead of one flat program vocabulary.
- Combine theory induction with intervention or counterfactual supervision.
- Compare against strong latent world models under matched data and compute.

### 12. Why does this matter for my work?
It matters because it offers a sharper criterion for structured world models: a useful latent should transfer as an explanation, not just as a predictor. That directly connects to compositional generation, modular world models, and reusable internal abstractions.

### 13. What ideas are steal-worthy?
- Evaluate latent structure by **transferable reuse**, not only predictive accuracy.
- Use shared execution machinery to pressure primitive disentanglement.
- Favor minimum-description explanations when multiple latent parses fit.
- Treat theory induction as a first-class objective rather than a side effect of representation learning.

### 14. Final decision
**Read first.** Even if the full vision is ahead of the current implementation, the paper is asking the right question and gives a mechanism worth thinking with.
